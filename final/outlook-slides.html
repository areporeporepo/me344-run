What This Run Measured,
And What It Will Mean Later

A dense 4B model on eight chips of a 2023 inference part. Most of what it measures is a property of the method, not the hardware, so it transfers. One finding is already dead. One scaled further than anything else here. This sorts them.

Scaled best of everything

AOT compile
Predicting memory before spending it looked like ceremony at 4B on 8 chips. MaxText now wraps the same primitive in an automated search running against DeepSeek3 671B on a tpu7x-512 pod, and you can drive it from a CPU. The technique got promoted, not retired.

Ages best

the fabric
ICI 100%, DCN 65%. The boundary moved from 256 chips on v5e to 9,216 on Ironwood, but it did not disappear: Ironwood ICI beats its own DCN by roughly 96x. And MoE puts a second collective on the critical path.

Already obsolete

the memory wall
gb512 failed at 18.43 GiB against 15.75 available. That is 10.3% of one Ironwood chip, so this specific wall is gone. The arithmetic that predicted it is what still says which models a fleet can train at all.

Regime, not fact

99% matmul
True at L=256, and only there. Attention scales as L² against matmul's L, so the same step is 97% attention at 1M context. That inversion is why 2026 architectures replaced most of their attention layers.
Model and dataset were fixed by the course: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, 4.022B dense, fine-tuned on PolyAI/banking77 (400 examples, 77 intents) with GSM8K as a retention check. Neither is the point. Read every accuracy number here as an instrument reading, not a capability claim.

Finding 2 · the one that ages best

The fabric is the scaling unit. The boundary moved; it did not vanish.

Measured here

16 chips
One ICI slice: 100.1% strong, 99.6% weak. Split across two slices so the all-reduce crosses DCN: 65.2% and 53.6%. Exposed communication 1.08 s to 3.51 s for a payload that never changed size.

On Ironwood

9,216 chips
The ICI domain grows 36x, so a 16-chip job never leaves ICI. The specific 35% penalty measured here does not arise at that scale.

Still true

~96x
Ironwood ICI is 1,200 GB/s per chip against 100 Gbps of DCN. Every multi-pod run still crosses it. Performance is a step function in where the collective travels, not a smooth function of chip count.

The anomaly, still unexplained

DCN weak came out worse per chip than DCN strong, 1,085 against 1,320 tokens/s/chip, which a fixed-size all-reduce should not do. Lost overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, and shared-fabric contention are all consistent. The GKE jobs saved no traces, so I cannot tell you which.

Google built silicon around this exact point

Dense training has one collective per step. MoE adds an all-to-all per layer, on every forward and backward pass. My prediction was that MoE across DCN degrades worse than 65.2%, and it is still untested by me. But TPU 8i drops the 3D torus for a high-radix topology that cuts network diameter from 16 hops to 7, for a claimed 50% better all-to-all latency, and Google names MoE serving as the reason. A vendor redesigning its interconnect to make all-to-all cheaper is the strongest signal available that this collective, not the gradient all-reduce, is what now decides performance.

Findings 3 and 4 · what the frontier actually looks like

99% dense matmul is a statement about L=256, not about transformers.

Attention FLOPs scale as L², weight matmul as L, so their ratio scales as L. Taking this run's measured 0.93 / 99.07 split and extending to a 1M context:
0.0094 × (1,000,000 / 256) = 36.7
→ attention becomes 97.3% of step FLOPs
The step inverts completely. This run measured the short-context corner where architecture choice is invisible. Both leading open MoE families replaced most of their attention precisely because of this:
  • Kimi K3 (2026-07-16): Kimi Delta Attention, linear layers interleaved with full attention 3:1, reported 6.3x faster decode at 1M context.
  • Qwen3.5: Gated DeltaNet replacing 75% of attention layers.
And essentially nothing at the frontier is dense any more. The trend toward extreme sparsity is monotone:

Findings 7 and 8 · the landscape this lands in

Three vendors now answer the fabric question differently, and the open models are the only ones you can measure.

This run's real question was: how many chips can you reach before falling off the fast fabric? That is now a design choice, and the answers differ by two orders of magnitude.

Google now ships two answers in one generation. 9,600 chips in a 3D torus for training, 1,152 in a high-radix fabric for serving, because the collective that matters differs. Note 8i, the inference part, has more HBM and more bandwidth than 8t, the training part. Beyond the superpod, the Virgo network claims 4x the prior scale-out DCN bandwidth and over 1 million chips in one training cluster via JAX and Pathways. Google is spending hard on exactly the boundary this run measured a 35% penalty at.
Zhenwu M890 is T-Head's PPU, announced with Qwen3.7-Max, racked 128-wide as the Panjiu AL128. If 128 is a real fabric boundary rather than a SKU, the wall this run hit at 16 chips arrives at 128 there, not 9,216. Unverified.

Alibaba went open to closed in about five weeks. Moonshot went the other way and shipped 2.8 trillion parameters in the open. Every projection on the previous slides needs a parameter count and loadable weights, so Kimi K3 is the reason this exercise is still possible at frontier scale at all.
A systems argument, not a licensing opinion: predict the memory before you spend it, find the knee, measure where the collective travels. All three need weights.

And the workload the silicon is shaped around is not this one

Google says 8t and 8i are "built to efficiently train and serve world models like Google DeepMind's Genie 3, enabling millions of agents to practice and refine their reasoning in diverse simulated environments." Genie 3 generates navigable worlds at 24 fps and 720p; Project Genie shipped 2026-01-29. That is neither the dense text pre-training measured here nor ordinary chat serving: it is simulation for agents to train inside. 8i's 288 GB of HBM and its on-silicon KV cache for long-context decoding read as built for exactly that. My prediction: the interesting systems question of 2027 is not tokens/s on a training step, it is throughput and latency of a simulate-act-learn loop, where the fabric question this run measured reappears between the model and the environment rather than between two model replicas.

Finding 9 · a correction to slide 2

Ahead-of-time compilation did not go away. It got automated and aimed at 671B.

What this run did by hand

2 shapes
Compiled TP8 and DP8 ahead of time at gb2 / L512 and predicted both outcomes before a training step ran. TP8: predicted fit, fit at 8.23 GiB/chip. DP8: predicted OOM, produced 65.47 GiB against 15.75 available. Felt like ceremony at 4B on 8 chips.

What MaxText does now

671B
train_compile.py still flags OOM "with an identical OOM stack trace as if it was compiled on the target hardware", and you can compile for a TPU cluster from a CPU. It is now wrapped in estimator.py, which searches remat policies automatically. The documented example: DeepSeek3 671B on a tpu7x-512 pod, finding the least aggressive policy that fits.

The honest limit

110%
gb256 was AOT-predicted at 17.37 GiB, 110% of the 15.75 GiB limit, and ran anyway at 16,227 tokens/s. Rematerialization absorbed the overshoot. AOT locates the boundary and tells you which side you are on. It is not an exact number, and this run has the counterexample in its own sweep.

Why this matters more at trillion scale, not less

  • The cost of finding out empirically scales with the cluster. Discovering an OOM 20 minutes into a 512-chip Ironwood job is real money. AOT moves it to a CPU.
  • MoE makes the boundary harder to reason about on paper: expert sharding, capacity factor, all-to-all buffers. The compiler's accounting is worth more when your own arithmetic is worth less.
  • SparseCore offloading and latency-hiding scheduling change the memory layout, so compiler flags move the OOM point. No closed form captures that.

So slide 2 was half wrong

Calling the memory wall obsolete is right about the number and wrong about the method. The 18.43 GiB that stopped this run is 10.3% of one Ironwood chip, so that specific wall is gone. But the technique that predicted it did not get retired, it got promoted into an automated search loop running against 671-billion-parameter MoE models. Of everything measured here, the capacity lab is the part that scaled best.

The finding with no expiry date

GRPO's accuracy effect came out +12.5 points here and -12.5 on an identical run three days earlier, guardrail flipping pass to fail. A two-example swing on 16 items. Frontier models are ranked to a tenth of a point on aggregate indices; whether those gaps clear their own intervals is the question cell 30 asks, and it is asked far too rarely.

What would change this deck

TPU 8t "Sunfish" and 8i "Zebrafish" ship late 2027 on TSMC 2nm, training and inference split for the first time. Every number quoted for them is a vendor target, not a measurement. Sub-8-bit training becoming standard would rescale the whole capacity table. Each of the five experiments above either confirms or kills a projection here.
