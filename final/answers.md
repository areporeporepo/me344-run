# ME344 Final Reflection

1. **Profile and scaling:** What did XProf show, and how did ICI versus DCN affect the scaling results?

XProf revealed a 24.94 TFLOP training step that is 99.07% learnable-weight matmul against only 0.93% attention at sequence length 256, showing this shape is a dense-matmul workload rather than an attention-bound one and that the fabric matters far more than the chip count, because doubling the experiment from 8 to 16 chips inside one ICI slice maintained 100.1% strong and 99.6% weak scaling efficiency (2,026 against 2,025 tokens/s/chip), whereas spreading the same 16 chips across two slices so the data-parallel gradient all-reduce had to cross DCN dropped the efficiency to 65.2% strong and 53.6% weak (1,320 and 1,085 tokens/s/chip).

2. **Training:** What changed after SFT or GRPO?

I found that SFT for 200 steps moved banking77 exact match from 70.0% to 92.5% on 40 held-out prompts while holding 100% format compliance and lifting GSM8K retention from 81.25% to 87.5%, and while applying GRPO on top increased format compliance from 37.5% to 93.75% and moved exact accuracy from 10/16 to 12/16, the held-out loss hit its best mark at step 50 (0.0436) before drifting to 0.0623 by step 200, which means the run's own stopping rule prefers rolling back instead of training further.

3. **Release:** Which checkpoint would you ship, and what evidence supports it?

I would ship the SFT checkpoint at step 202 because it is the only candidate offering a domain gain with no retention regression and a clear serving measurement (70.0% to 92.5% banking77 exact match, 81.25% to 87.5% GSM8K retention, 27.0 requests/s at concurrency 1 and 75.5 requests/s at concurrency 4), and I would explicitly not lean on the GRPO checkpoint because its +12.5 point accuracy change comes from just two of the 16 items and came out -12.5 on an identically configured earlier run and is not trusted, especially since the 95% Wilson interval on the SFT result (80.1% to 97.4%) still overlaps the base interval (54.6% to 81.9%), making me treat this whole run as a release smoke test rather than a launch decision.

4. **Next step:** What would you try next with more time or a dataset you care about?

With more time I would stop measuring a dense 4B model at sequence length 256 because both of the things that make these numbers interesting are artifacts of that choice, since the step is 99.07% weight matmul only because attention is negligible at that length and the once-per-step gradient all-reduce that cost 35% crossing DCN is the cheap collective next to the per-layer expert all-to-all that a sparse model adds, meaning I would re-run this same scaling harness on an MoE checkpoint such as Qwen3-30B-A3B at long context and expect the DCN penalty to be worse than 65.2%, but before trusting any of that I need to close the two gaps this run left: re-run the two DCN trials with XProf enabled, because the weak-scaling anomaly (1,085 against 1,320 tokens/s/chip for a payload that never changes size, 3.51 s of exposed communication against 1.08 s) leaves the DCN cause undetermined between lost compute/communication overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, and shared-fabric contention, and grow the evaluation suite past the 97 examples the Wilson analysis requires for a plus-or-minus 10 point margin on a dataset that matters rather than a 2020 intent-classification set, then finally evaluate the step 50 checkpoint the stopping rule prefers, which no task metric has ever been run against.
