Train, Tune, And Scale Qwen3-4B
on a TPU v5e-8

Forty minutes and $5.96 on eight v5e chips, spent answering three questions: what fits, what is worth running, and what breaks when you add more chips.

What fits

TP8, not DP8
Tensor parallel shards the 16 bytes/parameter of training state eight ways and fits in 8.23 GiB/chip. Data parallel replicates it and needs 59.94, so it dies at 65.47 GiB against 15.75 available. I predicted both before running them.

What is worth running

global batch 32
Throughput saturates there at 16,562 tokens/s using 59% of HBM. The memory wall is four doublings later at gb512. Everything between spends memory for nothing.

What breaks

the fabric
16 chips inside one ICI slice: 100% efficiency. The same 16 chips across two slices, so the gradient all-reduce crosses DCN: 65% strong, 54% weak.

And one result I cannot explain

3.2×
DCN weak is worse per chip than DCN strong, which a gradient all-reduce sized by the model rather than the batch should not do. Differencing against ICI at matched batch: 3.51 s exposed against 1.08 s, same payload. Lost overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, or contention from other students all fit, and the GKE jobs saved no traces. Naming one would be a guess.

Conclusion

What I would ship, and what this pod says about bigger models

Ship SFT step 202

  • Only candidate with a domain gain, no retention regression, and a serving measurement: 27.0 req/s at concurrency 1, 75.5 at concurrency 4.
  • Not the GRPO checkpoint, whose accuracy effect flipped sign between runs.
  • Treated as a smoke test, not a launch: the intervals overlap and 97 examples are needed for a real margin.
  • Open question I am not hiding: the stopping rule prefers step 50, which no task metric has ever scored.

Two measurements that generalize

16 bytes/parameter of training state, validated against this run to the second decimal. It says a 2.8T-parameter model needs about 2,650 v5e chips, and a slice caps at 256, so frontier-scale training is forced across the DCN where I measured a 35 to 46% penalty. The fabric, not the chip count, is the binding constraint at scale.
0.93% attention at L=256. Attention grows as L² against L for the weights, so it reaches parity near 27,000 tokens of context and would be ~97% of the step at a million. Every conclusion on these slides is a short-sequence result. Long-context and world-model training is a different machine problem on the same chips.
The habit worth keeping: the run's artifacts were destroyed once when a VM was deleted. Everything here was rebuilt from Cloud Storage plus a re-run, which is why every slide names the file it came from.
