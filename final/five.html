on a TPU v5e-8

Forty minutes on eight v5e chips, 5.0 chip-hours, spent answering three questions: what fits, what is worth running, and what breaks when you add more chips.

What fits

TP8, not DP8

Tensor parallel shards the 16 bytes per parameter of training state eight ways and fits in 8.23 GiB per chip. Data parallel replicates it, needs 59.94, and dies at 65.47 against 15.75 available. Both outcomes predicted before running either.

What is worth running

global batch 32

Throughput saturates at 16,562 tokens/s using 59.4% of HBM. The memory wall is four doublings later at gb512. Everything in between spends memory for nothing.

What breaks

the fabric

16 chips inside one ICI slice: 100% efficiency. The same 16 across two slices, so the gradient all-reduce crosses DCN: 65% strong, 54% weak.

Why the chip was only a quarter busy, and both answers matter in 2027

First, the batch does not save you

Throughput saturates at global batch 32 (16,562 tokens/s, 59.4% of HBM) and the first shape that fails to compile is gb512 (18.43 GiB against 15.75). Between them sit four doublings that spend memory for nothing, and MFU never moves off 25.5% across that whole range. A sequence sweep to 2048 moves it under half a point too. So the ceiling is not something you tune your way out of.

Isolated, in two parts

Across chips it is bandwidth. v6e has 4.66x the compute of v5e but delivered 2.16x, almost exactly its 2.00x bandwidth ratio, and MFU fell to 11.6%. MFU rank-orders perfectly by bytes per FLOP: v5p 6.02 to 31.7%, v5e 4.16 to 25.1%, v6e 1.79 to 11.6%. v5e to v6e doubles HBM, bandwidth and ICI but multiplies compute by 4.66.

Within a chip it was my sharding. TP8 all-reduces activations at every layer, so its traffic scales with tokens and no batch or sequence setting escapes it, which is why this curve is flat across an 8x range. FSDP8 ships weights instead, a fixed cost per step: same chips, same memory, 52.7% MFU. The ceiling was a choice, not the hardware.

3 of 4 · scaling

Chip count is not the scaling unit. The fabric is.

Inside one ICI slice: free

8 to 16 chips holds 100.1% strong and 99.6% weak efficiency. Per chip throughput goes 2,025 to 2,026. Replicated on v6e on 2026-08-14: 8 to 16 chips gave 97.1% strong scaling, 1.870 s to 0.962 s. The result is not specific to v5e.

Across DCN: 35% gone

Same model, same batches, same logical layout. Only the fabric the gradient all-reduce travels over changed.

One result I cannot explain

DCN weak is worse per chip than DCN strong, 1,085 against 1,320. An all-reduce payload is fixed by parameter count, so doubling the batch should not hurt.

Three candidates, no evidence

Lost compute/communication overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, or shared-fabric contention. The GKE jobs saved no traces.

4 of 4 · outlook

What I would ship, and what this pod says about 2027

Ship SFT step 202

The only candidate with a domain gain, no retention regression, and a serving measurement behind it. Not GRPO: its gain did not reproduce.

The arithmetic outlives the number

16 bytes per parameter is what says which models a fleet can train. Kimi K3 needs 11x an entire v5e pod. That is why 2.8T open models arrived in 2026.

The fabric result ages best

On Ironwood the boundary moves from 256 chips to 9,216 but does not vanish. TPU 8i drops the 3D torus for a topology cutting 16 hops to 7, claiming 50% better all-to-all, and names MoE serving as the reason.

99% matmul is about L=256

Attention scales as L² against matmul's L, so at 1M context the same step is 97% attention. That is why Kimi K3 and Qwen3.5 replaced most of their attention.

Prediction, not measurement

Google says 8t and 8i were built for world models like Genie 3. If so, 2027's systems question is a simulate-act-learn loop, not tokens/s on a training step.
