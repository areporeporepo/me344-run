Outlook: What This Run Measured, And What It Will Mean Later

Written 2026-08-13, from the measurements in run qanh-20260813t194806z.

The assignment measures a dense 4B model on 8 chips of a 2023 inference part. That sounds narrow. It is not: most of what it measures are properties of the method, and they transfer. One finding is already obsolete. One turns out to have scaled better than anything else here. This page separates them, projects each onto hardware and architectures that exist now or are announced, and records what would have to be re-measured to check the projection.

On the model and the dataset, briefly. The run fine-tunes Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, a dense 4.022B-parameter model, on PolyAI/banking77 (400 training examples, 77 intent labels, CC BY 4.0, revision pinned), with openai/gsm8k test[0:16] as a retention check. Both were fixed by the course, not chosen here, and neither is the point. banking77's job in this assignment is to be a task simple enough that a small model measurably changes behavior in 200 steps, so the systems work around it can be measured cleanly. It does that job well, which is a compliment to whoever picked it. Every accuracy number in this project should be read as an instrument reading rather than a capability claim. The rest of this page is about the instrument.

Everything derived here starts from a number this run actually produced. Vendor specs are from Google Cloud's own documentation, cited at the bottom, and dated. Where I am guessing, it says so.

Announced but not shipping: the eighth generation splits into two parts for the first time. Both TSMC 2nm, both late 2027. Those are vendor targets, not measurements, and no one outside Google has run anything on either.

Finding 1: the memory wall. Obsolete on v7, and that is the point

What this run measured. Global batch 512 failed to compile: 18.43 GiB of temporaries against 15.75 GiB available. The replicated DP8 layout failed harder, needing 65.47 GiB. Only TP8 fit, at 7.49 GiB per chip. Unsharded peak before activations was 59.94 GiB.

On Ironwood. 18.43 GiB is 19.8 GB, which is 10.3% of one Ironwood chip. The full 59.94 GiB unsharded state is 64.4 GB, 33.5% of one chip. The entire capacity lab, the exercise of predicting whether a shape fits before paying to find out, resolves to "fits, on one chip, with no tensor parallelism at all."

Why that does not make the exercise pointless. The method survives; only the model is too small. Apply this run's own measured constant, 16 bytes of training state per parameter, to what people actually train now:

Model Params State at 16 B/param Ironwood chips to hold it v5e pod (256 chips, 4 TB)
Qwen3-4B (this run) 4.0B 64 GB 0.34 fits
Qwen3-30B-A3B 30.5B 488 GB 2.5 fits
Qwen3-235B-A22B 235B 3.8 TB 20 barely, 94% of pod HBM
Kimi K2 1T 16 TB 84 impossible, 4x the whole pod
Kimi K3 2.8T 44.8 TB 233 impossible, 11x the whole pod

An entire 256-chip v5e pod holds 4 TB of HBM. Kimi K3's training state at this run's measured byte count needs 44.8 TB. That is the real content of the memory wall finding: not that gb512 failed, but that the arithmetic which predicted it is the arithmetic that says which models a given fleet can train at all. An Ironwood pod is 1.77 PB and a TPU 8t superpod is a claimed 2 PB, so both clear 44.8 TB with room, which is why 2.8T-parameter open models appeared in 2026 and not in 2023.

What to re-measure. 16 B/param was measured under BF16 with a specific optimizer. It is the most perishable number here. See finding 5.

Finding 2: the fabric is the scaling unit. This is the finding that ages best

What this run measured. 8 to 16 chips inside one ICI slice: 100.1% strong, 99.6% weak. The same 16 chips split across two slices so the gradient all-reduce crosses DCN: 65.2% and 53.6%. Exposed communication went from 1.08 s to 3.51 s at matched batch, a 3.2x jump for a payload that never changed size.

On Ironwood. The ICI domain goes from 256 chips to 9,216. A 16-chip job never leaves ICI. The specific 35% penalty this run measured simply does not arise at that scale.

Why the finding survives anyway. The boundary moved, it did not disappear. Every multi-pod training run still crosses DCN, and Ironwood's DCN is 100 Gbps per chip against 1,200 GB/s of ICI, a ratio of roughly 96x. The lesson generalizes exactly: performance is a step function in where the collective travels, not a smooth function of how many chips you bought. What changed is the chip count at which you hit the step, from 16 to 9,216.

This is also the finding that transfers most directly to MoE, below, because MoE moves a second collective onto the critical path.

The anomaly is still unexplained and still worth chasing. DCN weak scaling came out worse per chip than DCN strong, 1,085 against 1,320 tokens/s/chip, which a fixed-size all-reduce should not do. The GKE jobs saved no traces. Lost overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, and shared-fabric contention are all consistent. Re-running those two trials with XProf enabled is the single highest-value follow-up in this whole project, and it costs maybe 20 minutes of TPU time.

Finding 3: the step is 99% dense matmul. True only in the regime measured

What this run measured. A 24.94 TFLOP step that is 99.07% learnable-weight matmul against 0.93% attention, at sequence length 256.

Why that ratio is not a fact about transformers. Attention FLOPs scale as L² while weight matmul scales as L, so the ratio between them scales as L. Taking this run's measured 0.93 / 99.07 = 0.0094 and scaling to a 1M-token context:

0.0094 x (1,000,000 / 256) = 36.7  ->  attention is 97.3% of step FLOPs

The step inverts completely. This run measured the short-context corner where architecture choice is invisible, and it is worth knowing that is what it measured.

Which is exactly why the frontier changed shape in 2026. Both leading open MoE families replaced most of their attention:

  • Kimi K3 (2026-07-16): Kimi Delta Attention, linear-attention layers interleaved with full attention at 3:1, reported 6.3x faster decoding at 1M context.
  • Qwen3.5: Gated DeltaNet replacing 75% of standard attention layers.

Both are engineering responses to the number above. If you re-ran this run's profile cell at L=131072 on a KDA-style model, the 99/1 split should collapse toward parity, and the profile would start showing linear-attention state updates rather than a single dominant matmul. That is a concrete, cheap experiment and nobody in the class ran it.

Finding 4: MoE moves the bottleneck to the collective this run already measured

This run trained a dense 4B. Essentially nothing at the frontier is dense any more:

Model Total Active Experts Date
Qwen3-30B-A3B 30.5B 3.3B (10.8%) 128, 8 active, no shared 2025
Qwen3-235B-A22B 235B 22B (9.4%) 128, 8 active 2025
Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 35B ~3B (~9%) 256, 8 routed + 1 shared 2026-04-16
Kimi K2 1T 32B (3.2%) 384 + 1 shared, MLA 2025-07
Kimi K3 2.8T not confirmed 896, 16 active (1.8%) 2026-07-16

The trend is monotone toward extreme sparsity: total parameters grow much faster than active ones. Two consequences follow directly from what this run measured.

First, HBM capacity binds before FLOPs do. A sparse model pays full HBM cost for every expert and compute cost for 2 to 11% of them. That is precisely why 192 GB per chip is the headline Ironwood number rather than the 11.7x compute jump, and why the finding-1 table above is the interesting one.

Second, expert-parallel all-to-all lands on the critical path, and finding 2 is the right instrument for it. Dense training has one collective per step, the gradient all-reduce this run measured. MoE adds an all-to-all per MoE layer, dispatching tokens to experts and gathering results back, on every forward and backward pass. It is latency-sensitive, irregular, and load-imbalanced when routing is skewed. The ICI-versus-DCN gap this run measured at 65.2% for a once-per-step all-reduce would be far more punishing for a per-layer all-to-all. Prediction: an MoE model split across DCN degrades substantially worse than the 65.2% measured here. I have not measured this and neither has anyone else in the class. It is testable with the same scale-job.yaml harness and a Qwen3-30B-A3B checkpoint.

Finding 5: 16 bytes per parameter. The number most likely to be wrong already

What this run measured. 16 bytes per parameter at peak, 12 persistent plus 4 transient, under BF16.

What changed. Ironwood runs FP8 at exactly 2x its BF16 rate, 4,614 against 2,307 TFLOP/s. Kimi K2 shipped with native INT4. Kimi K3 uses MXFP4/MXFP8. Low-precision training and inference are no longer research, they are the default path on current silicon.

Every capacity projection in finding 1 scales linearly with this constant, so getting it wrong scales the whole table wrong. If sub-8-bit training states become standard, the 233 Ironwood chips projected for Kimi K3 could be closer to 60.

What to re-measure. Re-run the capacity lab with FP8 enabled and recover the byte count empirically, the same way this run did. The AOT compile path already reports it. This is maybe an hour of work and it re-grounds the entire table.

Finding 6: the evaluation was too small. This one is timeless and mostly ignored

Not a hardware finding, and the one most likely to still be true in ten years.

This run showed GRPO's accuracy effect coming out +12.5 points on 2026-08-13 and -12.5 points on an identically configured run three days earlier, with the guardrail flipping pass to fail. A two-example swing on a 16-item set. The primary suite's Wilson intervals still overlap by 1.8 points and would need 97 examples for a plus-or-minus 10 point margin.

Frontier models are ranked on aggregate indices to a tenth of a point. Kimi K3 was reported at 57.1 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1, against 58.9 and 59.9 for the models above it. Whether those gaps clear their own confidence intervals is exactly the question this run's cell 30 asks, and it is asked far less often than it should be. The habit of computing the interval before believing the delta is the most portable thing in this assignment.

Finding 7: three vendors are now answering the fabric question differently

This run's ICI-versus-DCN result is really one question: how many chips can you reach before you fall off the fast fabric onto something slower? That number is now a design choice each vendor is making differently, and it is the single most useful axis for comparing accelerators against what this run measured.

v5e (this run) Ironwood v7 TPU 8t (late 2027) Zhenwu M890
HBM per chip 16 GB 192 GB 8 stacks, 12-high HBM3e 144 GB HBM3
Inter-chip bandwidth 400 GB/s 1,200 GB/s ~30% above Ironwood 800 GB/s
Fast-domain size 256 chips 9,216 chips 9,600 chips 128 per Panjiu AL128 rack
Status measured here shipping vendor target announced 2026-05-20

Alibaba announced the Zhenwu M890 on 2026-05-20, the same day as Qwen3.7-Max: an in-house PPU (Parallel Processing Unit) architecture from T-Head with a Transformer core engine, claiming 3x the previous Zhenwu 810E, packaged into a Panjiu AL128 rack that links 128 accelerators as one unit. Alibaba also reported Qwen3.7-Max running autonomously for about 35 hours across 1,158 tool calls to optimize kernels for that chip, claiming a 10x geometric-mean speedup over a reference Triton kernel. No independent reproduction of that had been published as of 2026-05-25.

The number worth staring at is 128. If the M890's fast domain really is one rack, then the boundary this run hit at 16 chips arrives at 128 there rather than 9,216. Same question, answers two orders of magnitude apart. I have not verified whether 128 is a hard fabric limit or just the shipping SKU, and that distinction decides whether the comparison above is fair.

Finding 8: the set of models you can actually measure is shrinking

Everything in the capacity table depends on knowing a parameter count and being able to load weights. You cannot run a capacity lab against an API endpoint. So it matters which way the frontier is moving:

Model Weights Date
Qwen3.6-35B-A3B open 2026-04-16
Qwen3.7-Max proprietary, API only 2026-05-20
Qwen3.7-Plus proprietary, API only 2026-06-01
Kimi K3, 2.8T open 2026-07-16

Alibaba went from open weights at 3.6 to API-only at 3.7, in about five weeks. Moonshot went the other way and shipped 2.8 trillion parameters in the open. The projections in this document work for Kimi K3 and the open Qwen3.x line and stop working the instant a model is API-only, which makes Kimi K3 the reason this exercise is still possible at frontier scale at all.

That is a systems argument, not a licensing opinion: the methods this assignment teaches, predict the memory before you spend it, find the throughput knee, measure where the collective travels, require access to weights. Whoever keeps publishing them decides who gets to do this work.

Finding 9: ahead-of-time compilation did not go away. It got automated and aimed at 671B

This is a correction to finding 1 as much as an addition.

The capacity lab is the part of this assignment that looks most like ceremony. You compile a shape without running it, read the compiler's memory accounting, and predict fit or failure. On a 4B model on eight chips that takes about a minute and feels like a formality.

It is the technique that scaled best of anything here.

What this run did. run_capacity_lab() compiled two layouts ahead of time at global batch 2, sequence length 512, and predicted both outcomes correctly before a single training step ran. TP8 predicted fit and fit, at 8.23 GiB per chip. DP8 predicted out of memory and produced exactly that, 65.47 GiB of temporaries against 15.75 GiB available.

What MaxText does with the same primitive now. train_compile.py still exists and still does precisely this. Per the current documentation it "will flag any out of memory (OOM) information, such as when the per_device_batch_size is set too high, with an identical OOM stack trace as if it was compiled on the target hardware." Three properties make it scale:

  • You can compile for a TPU cluster from a CPU. No TPU is needed to discover that a config will not fit. The documented example compiles a 16B model for two v5e pods from a single VM.
  • Compiled artifacts save and reload, so the compile cost is paid once and startup and restart get faster.
  • It has been wrapped in an automated search. MaxText now ships estimator.py, which drives train_compile in a loop to find a workable configuration without target hardware. The documented example runs it against DeepSeek3 671B on a tpu7x-512 pod, in policy-only mode, searching for "the least aggressive remat policy that fits."

So the thing done by hand here at 4B on 8 chips is now an automated search loop at 671B on 512 Ironwood chips. Same primitive, three orders of magnitude apart.

Why it matters more at trillion scale, not less. Three reasons, and they compound:

  1. The cost of finding out empirically scales with the cluster. Discovering an OOM twenty minutes into a 512-chip Ironwood job is expensive in a way that discovering it on eight v5e chips is not. AOT moves that discovery onto a CPU.
  2. MoE makes the memory boundary harder to reason about on paper. Expert sharding, capacity factor and all-to-all buffers all move it. The compiler's own accounting is worth more when your own arithmetic is less trustworthy, which is exactly the sparse-model case.
  3. Compiler flags move the boundary. SparseCore collective offloading and latency-hiding scheduling change the memory layout, so they change where OOM happens. No closed-form estimate captures that. You have to ask the compiler.

The honest limit, which this run also measured. Global batch 256 was AOT-predicted at 17.37 GiB, which is 110% of the 15.75 GiB limit, and it ran anyway at 16,227 tokens/s. The AOT figure is a compile-time estimate, not a runtime guarantee, and rematerialization absorbed the overshoot. So AOT locates the boundary approximately and tells you which side of it you are on. It does not give you an exact number, and this run has the counterexample in its own sweep table.

Correcting finding 1. Calling the memory wall obsolete is right about the number and wrong about the method. The 18.43 GiB that stopped this run is 10.3% of one Ironwood chip. The technique that predicted it got promoted into an automated search over 671B-parameter models.

What would change this page

  • TPU 8t/8i shipping in late 2027. Every number in the hardware table for those parts is a vendor target. Replace with measurements when they exist.
  • A confirmed active-parameter count for Kimi K3. Currently unavailable, which is why the table says so rather than guessing.
  • Sub-8-bit training becoming standard. Rescales finding 1 entirely.
  • Independent Ironwood utilization numbers. This run plateaued at 25.5% of v5e peak. Whether v7 does better on real training workloads is the biggest unknown here and nothing above answers it.
  • Whether the Zhenwu M890's 128-accelerator rack is a hard fabric boundary. Decides whether finding 7's comparison is fair.
  • Any of the five experiments above. Each one either confirms or kills a projection here.
