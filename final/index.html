The three results worth arguing about

1. The useful batch and the memory wall are not the same shape. Throughput saturates at global batch 32 (16,562 tokens/s, 59.4% of HBM), while the first shape that fails to compile is global batch 512. Four doublings of headroom sit between "as fast as this gets" and "out of memory", and everything in between spends HBM for nothing: gb64 buys 0.4% more throughput for 13% more memory.

512AOT out of memory: needs 18.43 GiB temporaries against 15.75 GiB available

2. The fabric is the scaling unit, not the chip count. Going from 8 to 16 chips inside one ICI slice holds 100.1% strong and 99.6% weak efficiency. Taking the same 16 chips and splitting them across two slices, so the data-parallel gradient all-reduce crosses DCN, drops that to 65.2% and 53.6%. And DCN weak is worse per chip than DCN strong (1,085 against 1,320 tokens/s/chip), which a fixed-size all-reduce should not do: differencing against ICI at matched batch shows 3.51 s of exposed communication against 1.08 s, a 3.2× jump for the same payload. Lost overlap, rematerialization at 110% of the AOT limit, and shared-fabric contention are all consistent with that, and the GKE jobs saved no traces, so I cannot tell you which it is.

3. The evaluation is too small to carry the release claim, and I can prove it. SFT moved banking77 exact match from 70.0% to 92.5% with no retention regression, which is the result I would ship. But GRPO's accuracy effect came out +12.5 points here and -12.5 points on an identically configured run three days earlier, with the guardrail flipping from pass to fail. That is a two-example swing on a 16-item set. The Wilson intervals say the same thing about the primary suite: base 54.6-81.9%, SFT 80.1-97.4%, still overlapping by 1.8 points, and 97 examples would be needed for a plus-or-minus 10 point margin.
