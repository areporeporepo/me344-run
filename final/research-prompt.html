Research Prompt: ME344 Outlook Refresh

Paste the block below into ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, or any research agent. Then hand the results back and they get folded into OUTLOOK.md and the outlook slides.

The prompt is written to produce something checkable. It demands dates, primary sources, and an explicit separation between measured numbers and vendor claims, because the existing outlook makes that distinction everywhere and the refresh has to preserve it.

The prompt

I have a Stanford HPC final project that measured a dense 4-billion-parameter model (Qwen3-4B) training on 8 Google TPU v5e chips, in August 2026. I want to know which of its findings still matter and which are dead, so I can keep the write-up alive rather than let it rot.

My measurements, which are the anchor for everything. Do not contradict these, they are mine:

  • Peak throughput 16,562 tokens/s at global batch 32, which is 59.4% of the HBM limit
  • Out of memory at global batch 512: needed 18.43 GiB against 15.75 GiB available
  • Sustained 25.5% of the v5e's 197 BF16 TFLOP/s peak, and it plateaued there across an 8x range of batch sizes
  • A training step is 24.94 TFLOPs, of which 99.07% is learnable-weight matmul and 0.93% is attention, at sequence length 256
  • Training state measured at 16 bytes per parameter at peak (12 persistent, 4 transient), under BF16
  • Scaling 8 to 16 chips inside one ICI slice: 100.1% strong efficiency, 99.6% weak
  • The same 16 chips split across two slices so the gradient all-reduce crosses DCN: 65.2% strong, 53.6% weak
  • An unexplained anomaly: DCN weak scaling was worse per chip than DCN strong (1,085 against 1,320 tokens/s/chip), with exposed communication jumping from 1.08 s to 3.51 s for an all-reduce payload that never changes size

Research these seven questions. Be specific, cite primary sources, and date every claim.

  1. TPU v8 (8t "Sunfish" and 8i "Zebrafish"). Everything currently known: per-chip BF16 and FP8 throughput, HBM capacity and bandwidth, ICI and DCN bandwidth, pod and superpod sizes, process node, ship date, and pricing. Flag clearly which figures are Google's targets versus independently measured. Has anything shipped or been benchmarked by a third party yet?

  2. Ironwood (TPU v7) in practice. Independent benchmarks, not Google's marketing. What utilization are real training workloads achieving as a fraction of the 2,307 BF16 TFLOP/s peak? My v5e run plateaued at 25.5%. Is the v7 number better, worse, or the same? What limits it?

  3. MoE communication cost, which is the biggest gap in my analysis. I measured a once-per-step gradient all-reduce. MoE adds an expert-parallel all-to-all per layer. I predict MoE across DCN degrades worse than my measured 65.2%, but I have not tested it. Find published measurements of MoE all-to-all overhead as a fraction of step time, on TPU or GPU, ideally split by whether the collective stays inside the fast fabric. Also: what do expert-parallel sharding strategies do to change this?

  4. The 16 bytes per parameter constant, which every capacity projection I make scales with. What is the equivalent figure for FP8 and for MXFP4/MXFP8 training as actually deployed in 2026? Is sub-8-bit training standard at the frontier now, or still research? Give measured optimizer and gradient state costs, not theory.

  5. Linear and hybrid attention at long context. Kimi K3 uses Kimi Delta Attention at a 3:1 linear-to-full ratio. Qwen3.5 replaced 75% of attention layers with Gated DeltaNet. I derived that a step which is 99.07% matmul at L=256 becomes roughly 97% attention at L=1M under quadratic attention. What do measured step profiles actually look like for these hybrid architectures at long context? Does the derivation hold, and where does it break?

  6. The accelerator landscape beyond NVIDIA and Google. Alibaba's Zhenwu M890 (PPU architecture, 144 GB HBM3, 800 GB/s inter-chip, Panjiu AL128 rack of 128), plus anything comparable from AWS Trainium, Cerebras, Groq, Huawei Ascend, or others. I care specifically about the numbers that map onto what I measured: HBM capacity and bandwidth per chip, inter-chip bandwidth, and the size of the fast-fabric domain before you fall off onto a slower network.

  7. Open weights versus API-only at the frontier. Qwen3.6 was open-weight; Qwen3.7 (Max and Plus) is API-only. Kimi K3 stayed open at 2.8T parameters. Is the trend toward closing or opening? This matters practically: capacity and scaling analysis of the kind I did is only possible on models whose weights you can actually obtain.

Output format. For each question: what is established and well-sourced, what is contested or vendor-claimed-only, and what is genuinely unknown. Give me a short list of the specific numbers that would most change my analysis if they turned out different from what I currently assume. Prefer primary sources: vendor documentation, papers, model cards, technical reports. Where you use secondary reporting, say so. If you cannot verify something, say you cannot rather than filling the gap.

Do not use em dashes or en dashes anywhere in your answer.
